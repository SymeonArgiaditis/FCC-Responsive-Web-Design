John F. Kennedy

1,000 days that reshaped a century

Here's a timeline of JFK's life:

  • 1917 Born in Brookline, Massachusetts, into the large, ambitious Kennedy family.
  • 1935-1936 Briefly attends Princeton, but illness forces him to withdraw.
  • 1936 Enrolls at Harvard University.
  • 1937 Tours Europe, gaining an early interest in foreign affairs as authoritarianism rises.
  • 1940 Graduates from Harvard; his senior thesis on Britain's unpreparedness for war is published as the book Why England Slept, becoming a bestseller.
  • 1941 Joins the US Navy despite chronic health problems, including a bad back.
  • 1943 Commands PT-109 in the Pacific. When a Japanese destroyer rams and sinks it, he leads survivors on a grueling swim to safety, towing an injured crewman by a life-jacket strap clenched in his teeth. He's later awarded the Navy and Marine Corps Medal.
  • 1944 His older brother Joseph P. Kennedy Jr., the family's original political heir, is killed when his bomber explodes on a volunteer mission — reshaping the family's ambitions around Jack.
  • 1946 Elected to the US House of Representatives from Massachusetts.
  • 1952 Elected to the US Senate, defeating incumbent Henry Cabot Lodge Jr.
  • 1953 Marries Jacqueline Bouvier.
  • 1954-1955 Undergoes dangerous spinal surgery for his chronic back pain; during a long recovery, he writes (with substantial help) Profiles in Courage, which wins the 1957 Pulitzer Prize for Biography.
  • 1960 Narrowly defeats Richard Nixon in the presidential election, becoming the youngest man elected president and the first Catholic president. The televised debates are seen as pivotal — radio listeners thought Nixon won, TV viewers favored Kennedy.
  • 1961 Inaugurated, delivering the famous line: "Ask not what your country can do for you—ask what you can do for your country." Authorizes the Bay of Pigs invasion of Cuba, which fails disastrously. Establishes the Peace Corps. Commits the US to landing a man on the Moon "before this decade is out."
  • 1962 Navigates the Cuban Missile Crisis, a 13-day standoff with the Soviet Union that brings the world to the brink of nuclear war, resolved through back-channel diplomacy.
  • 1963 (June) Delivers his "Ich bin ein Berliner" speech in West Berlin and a landmark civil rights address, calling civil rights "a moral issue."
  • 1963 (November 22) | Assassinated while riding in a motorcade through Dallas, Texas. Lee Harvey Oswald is arrested and charged, but is himself shot dead two days later by Jack Ruby before facing trial — fueling decades of conspiracy theories.
